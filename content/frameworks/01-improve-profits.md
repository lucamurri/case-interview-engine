## Use it when

Profits, margins or returns are falling (or not growing as expected).

## The core logic

- **Profit = Revenue − Costs**
  - **Revenue = Volume × Price**
    - Volume: market size × share — or customers × purchases per customer
    - Price: list price, discounts, mix of products or customers
  - **Costs = Fixed + Variable**
    - Variable: cost per unit × volume (materials, labor per unit, shipping, cloud usage)
    - Fixed: overhead, rent, salaries, sales force, R&D

Work **quantitatively**: get the numbers for two periods, find which line moved the most, then dig into that line. A simple **profit bridge** (volume effect + price effect + cost effects = total change) makes the answer crisp.

## Questions that unlock it

- Is it just us or the whole industry? (Internal vs. external cause.)
- When did it start, and what changed then?
- Is it one product, region, channel or customer segment?

## Adapt it

- **Pharma:** separate *list* price from *net* price — rebates, copay support and government discounts ("gross-to-net") often explain the whole story.
- **Healthcare providers:** revenue depends on **payer mix** (commercial vs. government vs. uninsured) and case mix, not just volume.
- **Tech / SaaS:** look at unit economics — customer acquisition cost vs. lifetime value, churn, and usage-driven costs such as AI inference.
- **Industrial:** high fixed costs mean volume drops hit profit hard (fixed-cost absorption); check aftermarket parts and service.
- **Retail / consumer:** product mix, promotions, waste/shrink and channel costs (e-commerce shipping, returns).

## Traps

- Listing every possible cost before looking at data. Ask for the numbers first.
- Saying "price fell" when *list* price rose but *net* price fell.
- Fixing the symptom ("cut costs") without identifying the cause.
- Forgetting that some "unprofitable" units feed profit elsewhere (an ED that fills hospital beds; a razor that sells blades).

## Coach's note

This is the question behind more cases than any other, and interviewers use it to test **discipline**. They want to see you ask for numbers early and find the line that moved before you brainstorm. A strong move is to say, "Before I list causes, can we look at revenue and costs over the last few years?" Then segment until the problem becomes obvious.
