title: "2. How do we grow the business?"
order: 2
summary: "Lay out every growth path, then size each one and pick the best two or three."
---

## Use it when

A client wants to grow revenue: "double sales in five years", "find new growth", "our core market is flattening".

## The core logic

Start with **where the growth comes from**, using a simple grid of products × customers:

| Products ↓ · Customers → | Existing customers | New customers |
|---|---|---|
| **Existing products** | Sell more: share gain, usage, price, retention | Reach new segments, regions or channels |
| **New products** | Cross-sell and upsell: adjacent products, services, bundles | Diversify: new business, often through acquisition |

Then decide **how to get there**: build it ourselves (organic), partner or license, or buy it (acquisition). Finally, **compare the options** on size of prize, time to impact, investment needed, fit with our capabilities, and risk.

## Questions that unlock it

- How much growth, by when? Is the target revenue, profit or market share?
- Which customers and products are growing fastest today?
- What does the client do better than anyone else that it could extend?

## Adapt it

- **Pharma:** label expansion (new indications), new geographies, lifecycle management (new formulations), in-licensing assets from biotechs.
- **Energy:** new customer segments (commercial, data centers), services on top of commodities (storage, efficiency, EV charging).
- **Tech / SaaS:** expansion revenue from existing accounts (seats, usage, premium tiers) is usually cheaper than winning new logos.
- **Consumer:** new channels (direct-to-consumer, retail partners), new occasions, premium or value versions of hero products.

## Traps

- Listing ideas without a way to prioritize them.
- Ignoring the cheapest growth: retention and pricing in the existing base.
- Proposing diversification without asking why this client would win there.

## Coach's note

A good growth answer ends in a **shortlist with numbers**: "Expanding into hospitals is worth ~$40M in three years and uses our existing sales team; the new product line is bigger but four years away." Interviewers want you to rank the options, not just list them.
