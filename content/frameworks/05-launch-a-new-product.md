---
title: "5. How should we launch a new product?"
order: 5
summary: "Check it's worth launching, decide who it's for, then plan how to win and how to deliver."
---

## Use it when

A client has a new product, drug, service or feature and asks whether and how to bring it to market.

## The core logic

1. **Is it worth launching?** Size of the opportunity, how much of it we can realistically capture, and the economics (price, unit cost, launch investment, break-even).
2. **Who is it for, and why us?** The target segment and their unmet need, our advantage over the alternatives they use today, and the risk of cannibalizing our own products.
3. **How do we win?** The go-to-market plan: positioning and message, price, channel, launch sequence (pilot, then scale) and timing.
4. **Can we deliver?** Supply and operations, regulatory approval, sales capacity, customer support.
5. **How will we know it's working?** A few launch metrics, such as adoption, repeat use or prescriptions, and the trigger to scale up or pull back.

## Questions that unlock it

- What problem does the product solve, and for whom?
- What do target customers use today, and why would they switch?
- Is there a deadline, such as a competitor launch, a season or a patent clock?

## Adapt it

- **Pharma:** the "customer" is three people: the prescriber, the payer and the patient. Market access (formulary position, price) is often the real launch.
- **Medtech:** hospital purchasing committees, training, and evidence that the product saves money.
- **Tech:** freemium or pilot programs, integrations, a land-and-expand sales motion.
- **Consumer:** retailer shelf space, trial (sampling, promotion) and repeat purchase.

## Traps

- Reciting the "4 Ps" without choosing a target segment first.
- Ignoring cannibalization of the client's existing products.
- Planning a national launch when a pilot would answer the big uncertainties first.

## Coach's note

Make the case for **one segment first**. Launches fail most often from trying to serve everyone. "I'd launch with X, because their need is most acute and we can reach them through our existing channel" is a strong, specific answer.
