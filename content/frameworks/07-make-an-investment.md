title: "7. Should we make this investment?"
order: 7
summary: "Does it fit, does it pay back, what are the alternatives, and can we execute it?"
---

## Use it when

A client is considering spending money upfront to earn returns later: a new plant, a technology system, a store refurbishment, a fleet upgrade, an R&D program.

## The core logic

1. **Strategic fit:** why this investment, why now? What problem does it solve, or what opportunity does it capture?
2. **Cash flows:**
   - Upfront cost (and any later reinvestment)
   - Incremental benefit each year: extra revenue × margin, plus cost savings
   - Timing: how fast the benefits ramp up, and how long they last
3. **Return versus hurdle:** payback period, or NPV / IRR if the interviewer wants it, compared with the client's target. Run a quick downside case.
4. **Alternatives:** do nothing, a smaller or phased version, leasing instead of buying, partnering.
5. **Execution and risks:** capability to deliver, disruption during the build, technology or demand risk.

## Questions that unlock it

- What is the client's hurdle (payback in X years, a minimum return)?
- Are the benefits new revenue, cost savings, or avoided losses?
- What happens if we *don't* invest? (The base case is often decline, not standing still.)

## Adapt it

- **Energy and infrastructure:** long asset lives, so the durability of revenue (contracts vs. market prices) drives the answer.
- **Pharma:** R&D investments are risk-adjusted by the probability of success.
- **Retail / consumer:** pilot in a few stores, measure the uplift, then roll out.
- **Tech:** benefits often come as productivity, so convert them into dollars (hours saved × cost per hour).

## Traps

- Comparing benefits with costs without considering timing.
- Using the "with investment" case against a "nothing changes" base case that isn't realistic.
- Forgetting the cost of running the new asset (maintenance, staff, licenses).

## Coach's note

A clean answer has **one decisive number and one key risk**: "It pays back in 4 years against a 5-year hurdle, but only if utilization reaches 70%. I'd want to test that assumption first." Phasing the investment is often the smartest recommendation.
