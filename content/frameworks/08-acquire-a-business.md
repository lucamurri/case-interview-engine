## Use it when

A client is considering buying a company, investing in a business, or (for private equity) whether a deal will hit its return target.

## The core logic

1. **Why do the deal?** Strategic rationale — growth, capabilities, filling a gap, scale. If there's no clear reason, stop here.
2. **Is the target a good business on its own?** Market, competitive position, profitability and its trend, risks.
3. **What is it worth to us?** Standalone value + synergies − integration costs. Compare with the price. The *premium* paid must be covered by synergies we can realistically capture.
4. **Can we capture it?** Integration difficulty, culture, key people, regulatory approval.
5. **How should we structure it?** Price, cash vs. shares, earn-outs or contingent payments that move risk to the seller.

## Quick valuation tools for interviews

- **Multiples:** value ≈ EBITDA × a multiple typical for the industry.
- **Risk-adjusted value (pipelines, projects):** value if successful × probability of success.
- **Private equity returns:** exit value ÷ equity invested = MOIC (money multiple). Roughly, 2x in 5 years ≈ 15% a year; 3x in 5 years ≈ 25% a year.

## Adapt it

- **Pharma:** value each drug by stage and probability; check patent life and whether the target fills a revenue gap.
- **Private equity:** returns come from EBITDA growth, multiple expansion and debt paydown — say which one the plan relies on.
- **Energy transition deals:** adjacent businesses (e.g. EV charging for a fuel retailer) may be worth more to this buyer than to anyone else — but don't pay the seller for value only you create.

## Traps

- Valuing the target without asking about the price.
- Counting synergies that you'd have to pay away, or that take years to arrive.
- Ignoring the "do nothing" or "build instead" alternatives.

## Coach's note

The cleanest test is one sentence: **the premium we pay must be smaller than the synergies we can realistically capture**. Get the standalone value, add believable synergies minus integration costs, and compare with the price. Then think about deal structure (earn-outs, contingent payments) to bridge the gap between buyer and seller.
