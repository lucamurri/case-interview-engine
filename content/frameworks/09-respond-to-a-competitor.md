title: "9. How should we respond to a competitive threat?"
order: 9
summary: "Understand the move, size what's at stake, then pick a response the competitor can't easily counter."
---

## Use it when

A competitor has done something that threatens the client: a price cut, a new product, a new entrant, a merger, or a disruptive business model.

## The core logic

1. **Understand the move:** what exactly did the competitor do, which customers is it aimed at, and why now? Is it sustainable for them (cost advantage, funding), or a short-term push?
2. **Size the threat to us:** which of our products and customers are exposed, how much revenue and profit is at risk, and how fast it could move. Separate loyal customers from price-sensitive ones.
3. **Lay out the options:**
   - Wait and monitor (if the threat is small or likely to fade)
   - Defend the core: match on price selectively, or strengthen loyalty (contracts, service, bundles)
   - Differentiate: compete on what the competitor can't easily copy
   - Respond elsewhere: counter in a market where the competitor is weaker
   - Partner or acquire, if the threat brings a capability we lack
4. **Evaluate each option:** cost to us, impact on the threat, and **how the competitor is likely to react**. Avoid starting a price war we can't win.

## Questions that unlock it

- How have customers reacted so far: any churn or lost bids?
- What is the competitor's cost structure compared with ours?
- What is our strongest, hardest-to-copy advantage?

## Adapt it

- **Pharma:** a new rival drug or a generic. Respond with clinical data, patient support, contracting, or a lifecycle move rather than a blanket price cut.
- **Tech:** a cheaper or bundled product from a big platform. Focus on the segments that value depth, integration or service.
- **Consumer / retail:** a discount entrant. Defend with a value line rather than cutting prices on the main brand.
- **Energy / utilities:** new low-cost entrants. Compete on reliability, long-term contracts and services.

## Traps

- Reacting to the headline without sizing the actual damage.
- Matching price across the whole portfolio when only one segment is at risk.
- Forgetting that the competitor will respond to *our* response.

## Coach's note

Interviewers love candidates who think **one move ahead**: "If we match the price, they can afford to cut again because their costs are 20% lower. So our best move is to lock in our largest customers with longer contracts and compete on service." Showing you've thought about the competitor's next step is what makes this answer stand out.
