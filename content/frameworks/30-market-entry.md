## Use it when

A client is considering a new country, customer segment, product category or business model.

## The core logic

1. **Is the market attractive?** Size, growth, profitability, how durable the profit pool is, regulation.
2. **Can we win?** Competitors and their reactions, customer needs, our capabilities and gaps, barriers to entry.
3. **Does it pay?** Revenue we could realistically capture, upfront investment, ongoing costs → break-even share, payback, or NPV vs. the client's hurdle.
4. **How should we enter?** Build ourselves, partner / license, joint venture, or acquire — and how fast.
5. **What could go wrong?** Key risks and how to test or mitigate them.

## Questions that unlock it

- What is the client's goal (growth, diversification, using spare capacity) and its hurdle (payback, share, revenue)?
- What does the client already have that helps (brand, technology, channels, customers)?
- How have other entrants done?

## Adapt it

- **Regulated markets (pharma, utilities, finance):** approval timelines and reimbursement or licensing can matter more than demand.
- **Consumer goods abroad:** distribution and local partners are often the deciding factor; price points may need redesign.
- **Energy / infrastructure:** revenue durability (contracts vs. merchant prices) and grid or site access dominate.
- **Tech:** switching costs and incumbents' bundling power; partnering with a platform can beat building.

## Traps

- Saying "yes, enter" without saying *how*.
- Using today's revenues when the market is about to get crowded.
- Ignoring the client's capabilities — an attractive market isn't automatically an attractive market *for this client*.
- No number: always compute a break-even share or payback and compare it with what's realistic.
