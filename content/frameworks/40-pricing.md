## Use it when

A client is launching a product, changing prices, or designing tiers, bundles or payment models.

## The core logic

Triangulate three views and explain where the price should sit between them:

- **Cost-based (the floor):** unit cost + target margin. Tells you the minimum, not the right answer.
- **Competitor-based (the reference):** prices of the closest alternatives, adjusted for differences in quality or features.
- **Value-based (the ceiling):** what the customer gains — money saved, revenue gained, health or time improved. Customers rarely pay all of it; you usually capture a share.

Then decide the **structure**: one price or tiers (good / better / best), per unit or subscription, upfront or over time, discounts by segment.

## Questions that unlock it

- What is the objective — revenue, profit, market share, access or ridership?
- Who decides, who pays and who uses? (Often three different people in healthcare and B2B.)
- How price-sensitive are customers (elasticity), and how will competitors react?

## Adapt it

- **Pharma & gene therapies:** value = avoided treatment costs + health gains; payers' budgets and risk drive structure (outcomes-based or annual payments).
- **B2B software:** price on a value metric that grows with the customer (seats, usage, transactions); fence tiers so customers don't trade down.
- **Consumer subscriptions:** anchor against the per-day or per-use cost; watch markup vs. margin confusion.
- **Public sector:** the objective may be ridership, equity or access, not revenue — design discounts for the groups you most want to serve.

## Traps

- Stopping at cost-plus.
- Ignoring elasticity: a higher price can lose more volume than it gains in margin.
- Forgetting cannibalization when adding a cheaper tier.
- Confusing markup (on cost) with margin (on price): a 40% markup is only a 29% margin.
