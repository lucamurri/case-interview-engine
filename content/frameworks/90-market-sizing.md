## Use it when

You're asked "how big is…", "how many…", or need a market size inside a larger case (entry, investment, capacity).

## The core logic

Pick **one** of two directions and say which you chose and why:

- **Demand side (top-down):** population → share that has the need → share that buys → units per buyer → price.
  *Good for consumer and patient markets.*
- **Supply side (bottom-up):** number of sellers or sites → capacity or sales per site → utilization → price.
  *Good when buyers are hard to count but suppliers aren't (cafés, factories, data centers).*

Then **segment** wherever behavior differs a lot: by customer type, age, income, company size, or usage intensity. One average across very different groups is the most common source of a wrong answer.

## Questions that unlock it

- Which geography, which year, units or value?
- Is this the whole market or only what our client can serve?
- Are repeat purchases or replacement cycles important (one-off vs. recurring)?

## Adapt it

- **Pharma & healthcare:** patients → diagnosed → treated → on this type of therapy → doses per year → net price. Persistence (how long patients stay on therapy) often halves the answer.
- **Energy:** installed base × replacement rate, or new capacity added per year. Distinguish power (MW) from energy (MWh).
- **Tech / SaaS:** organizations × seats per organization × adoption × price per seat per month × 12.
- **B2B / industrial:** plants or sites × equipment per site ÷ equipment lifetime = annual replacement demand.

## Traps

- Not saying your approach before calculating.
- Forgetting ×12 for monthly prices or ÷ lifetime for durable goods.
- Giving a number with no **sanity check** (compare with a known anchor, or size it a second way).
- Stopping at the number. Always say what it means for the client's decision.

## Coach's note

Market sizing isn't a question type of its own in the top 10, but it shows up inside almost all of them (entry, launch, investment). Treat it as a tool: state your approach, segment where behavior differs, round aggressively, and **sanity-check** with a second method or a known anchor.
